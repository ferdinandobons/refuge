Share what you know with your family, at school, with friends and colleagues: spreading information that helps reduce risk is a collective responsibility to which we must all contribute.

## Right now

If you live, work or stay in an area at risk

- Find out about your municipality's emergency plan to learn which areas are at risk of flooding, and where the escape routes and safe areas in your town are

- If you have any doubts about the safety of your home, consult an experienced engineer or building expert

## During a flood downstream of a large dam

If you are in a municipality downstream of a large dam and there is an alert for "collapse" or for the activation of water-release operations, the area where you are could be affected by a flood. In the first case, a public alert will be sent to your phone (cell broadcast); in the second, you may be warned by sirens or by other alarm systems that your municipality may have in place.

In both cases, the civil protection authorities may tell you to leave your home temporarily or give you other information on how to behave. In the absence of specific instructions, remember:

### If you are outdoors, near a watercourse downstream of a large dam

- Quickly get to the nearest higher ground.

- Move away from the flooded area: because of the speed at which the water flows, even a few centimetres could knock you over.

- Avoid using your car. Even a few centimetres of water could make you lose control of the vehicle or cause it to stall: you risk being trapped.

- Avoid underpasses, riverbanks and bridges: stopping in or passing through these places can be very dangerous.

- Warn the people around you of the imminent danger.

- Limit your use of your mobile phone: keeping the lines free helps the emergency services.

- Keep informed about how the situation is developing and follow the instructions given by the authorities.

### If you are indoors

- If you are in a basement or on the ground floor, go up to the upper floors. Avoid the lift: it may get stuck.

- Do not go down into cellars, basements or garages to save your belongings: you are risking your life.

- Under no circumstances go outside to move your car to safety.

- Help elderly people and people with disabilities who are in the building.

- Do not drink tap water: it may be contaminated.

- Turn off the gas and switch off the electricity. Do not touch electrical systems or appliances with wet hands or feet.

## After a flood downstream of a large dam

- Follow the instructions of the authorities before taking any action, such as returning home, shovelling mud, pumping water out of cellars, etc.

- Check whether you can turn the gas and electricity back on. If necessary, ask an expert for advice.

- Before drinking tap water, make sure that no orders or notices from your municipality forbid it; do not eat food that has come into contact with the floodwater, as it may be contaminated.

- Before using the drains, make sure that the sewers, septic tanks and wells are not damaged.

- Do not travel along flooded roads: there may be sinkholes, potholes, open manholes or severed power cables. In addition, the water may be polluted by fuel or other substances.

- Also be careful in areas where the water has receded: the road surface may be weakened and give way.
