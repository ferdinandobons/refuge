Learning how to prevent and reduce the effects of a flood is a task that concerns all of us.

Share what you know with your family, at school, with friends and colleagues: spreading information about flood risk is a collective responsibility to which we must all contribute.

## Right now

### You too, with simple actions, can help reduce flood risk.

- Respect the environment and report to your municipality any abandoned bulky waste, blocked drains, partially obstructed watercourses, etc.

- Find out about your municipality's emergency plan to learn which areas are prone to flooding, and where the escape routes and safe areas in your town are.

- If you or a member of your family need special assistance (people with disabilities, elderly people), let your municipality know.

- Keep informed about the alerts issued by the authorities and about the actions taken by your municipality.

- Prepare an emergency kit containing, for example, copies of your documents, a first aid kit, a torch, a battery-powered radio, etc.

- Avoid keeping valuables in the cellar or basement.

## During the alert

### At this stage, when an alert is issued, you can take some preventive actions to reduce the risk.

- Keep informed about the dangers expected in your area and about the measures taken by your municipality.

- Do not sleep in basements and avoid staying in them.

- Protect rooms at street level and close the doors of cellars, basements or garages only if this does not expose you to danger.

- If you have to travel, check your route first and avoid areas that may flood.

- Think carefully before moving your car or other belongings to safety: it can be dangerous.

- Share what you know about the alert and about the right way to behave.

- Check that your child's school is aware of the current alert and is ready to activate its emergency plan.

## During the flood

### If you are indoors

- Do not go down into cellars, basements or garages to save your belongings: you are risking your life.

- Under no circumstances go outside to move your car to safety.

- If you are in a basement or on the ground floor, go up to the upper floors. Avoid the lift: it may get stuck.

- Help elderly people and people with disabilities who are in the building.

- Limit your use of your mobile phone: keeping the lines free helps the emergency services.

- Keep informed about how the situation is developing and follow the instructions given by the authorities.

- Turn off the gas and switch off the electricity. Do not touch electrical systems or appliances with wet hands or feet.

- Do not drink tap water: it may be contaminated.

### If you are outdoors

- Move away from the flooded area: because of the speed at which the water flows, even a few centimetres could knock you over.

- Quickly get to the nearest higher ground, avoiding slopes or artificial embankments that could collapse.

- Take shelter on the upper floors of a building.

- Avoid using your car. Even a few centimetres of water could make you lose control of the vehicle or cause it to stall: you risk being trapped.

- Avoid underpasses, riverbanks and bridges: stopping in or passing through these places can be very dangerous.

- Watch where you walk: there may be sinkholes, potholes, open manholes, etc.

## After the flood

- Follow the instructions of the authorities before taking any action, such as returning home, shovelling mud, pumping water out of cellars, etc.

- Check whether you can turn the gas and electricity back on. If necessary, ask an expert for advice.

- Before using the drains, make sure that the sewers, septic tanks and wells are not damaged.

- Do not travel along flooded roads: there may be sinkholes, potholes, open manholes or severed power cables. In addition, the water may be polluted by fuel or other substances.

- Also be careful in areas where the water has receded: the road surface may be weakened and give way.

- Before drinking tap water, make sure that no orders or notices from your municipality forbid it; do not eat food that has come into contact with the floodwater, as it may be contaminated.
