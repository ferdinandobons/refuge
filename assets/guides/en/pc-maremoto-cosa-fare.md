Knowing the environment where you live, work or stay is important to react better in an emergency. Learning how to prevent and reduce the effects of a tsunami is a task that concerns all of us.

Share what you know with your family, at school, with friends and colleagues: spreading information about tsunami risk is a collective responsibility to which we must all contribute.

## Right now

### If you live, work or stay in an area at risk of tsunami

- Find out about your municipality's emergency plan to learn which areas are dangerous, the evacuation routes and times, the signs to follow and the assembly areas to reach in an emergency.

- Find out about the safety of your home and of the places around it.

- Make sure that your school or workplace has an evacuation plan and that regular drills are held.

- Prepare for an emergency with your family and make a plan for reaching the escape routes and assembly areas.

- Keep supplies of water and food at home and prepare an emergency kit containing, for example, copies of your documents, a first aid kit, a torch, a battery-powered radio, etc.

## During the tsunami

### If you are on the beach or in a coastal area

If you receive an alert message warning that a tsunami wave may arrive, or if you notice at least one of these signs:

- a strong earthquake that you felt yourself or heard about;

- a sudden and unusual withdrawal of the sea, a rapid rise in sea level or a large wave stretching across the whole horizon;

- a deep, growing roar coming from the sea, like that of a train or a low-flying aircraft.

- Move away and quickly get to the nearest higher ground (for example a hill or the upper floors of a building).

- Warn the people around you of the imminent danger.

- Run, following the fastest escape route.

- Do not use your car: it could become a trap.

### If you are on a boat

You may not notice the signs that come with the arrival of a tsunami, so it is important to always listen to radio announcements.

- If you are on a boat and have heard of an earthquake on the coast or at sea, head out to open sea.

- If you are in harbour, leave the boat and get to safety on high ground.

## After the tsunami

- Stay in the area you have reached and try to discourage anyone who wants to go back towards the coast: the first wave may be followed by other, more dangerous ones.

- Check the health of the people around you and, if possible, give first aid.

- Follow the instructions of the authorities to know when to leave the place where you are and what to do.

- Use the phone only when really necessary.

- Do not drink tap water.

- Do not eat food that has come into contact with the water and with the materials carried by the tsunami: it may be contaminated.

- If your home has been affected by the tsunami, do not go back in before you are authorised to do so.

**A tsunami can be caused by an earthquake or by volcanic activity: so also find out what to do in the event of an earthquake or an eruption.**
