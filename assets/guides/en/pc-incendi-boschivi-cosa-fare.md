Ask your municipality for information about its emergency plan to find out what it provides for wildfires: if there is none, ask for one to be drawn up so that you know how to behave.

Share what you know with your family, at school, with friends and colleagues: spreading information about wildfire risk is a collective responsibility to which we must all contribute.

## Right now

### You too, with simple actions, can prepare to deal better with a possible emergency.

- Find out about your municipality's emergency plan to learn what it provides for wildfires.

- Remember that in a wildfire a prompt response is essential. If you spot a fire, or even just smoke, you can contact: the fire service; the European emergency number (112); any dedicated local numbers. Then state the location precisely, specifying the municipality of the area affected by the fire and any other details useful for locating it.

## To prevent a wildfire

- Do not throw away lit cigarettes or matches: they can set dry grass on fire.

- Do not light fires in the woods outside designated areas. Never leave a fire unattended and make sure it is out before you leave.

- If you have to park your car, make sure the exhaust is not in contact with dry grass: it could catch fire.

- Do not leave rubbish in the woods or in illegal dumps: it is a dangerous fuel.

- Do not burn stubble, straw or other agricultural waste without the proper safety measures. Within a few minutes you could lose control of the fire.

- If it is windy, never light a fire.

## If you spot a wildfire

- If you see flames, or even just smoke, call the fire service or the European emergency number 112 immediately. Do not assume that someone else has already done so.

- Give the information needed to locate the fire.

## If you are caught in a wildfire

- Look for a safe escape route: a road or a watercourse. Do not stop in places towards which the wind is blowing. You could be trapped by the flames.

- Lie down on the ground in a place where there is no vegetation that can burn. Smoke tends to rise, so this way you avoid breathing it in.

- If you have no other choice, try to cross the fire where it is least intense to get to the area that has already burned. This takes you to a safe place.

- Do not stop along the road: you would obstruct the emergency services.
