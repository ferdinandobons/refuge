Share what you know with your family, at school, with friends and colleagues: spreading information that helps reduce risk is a collective responsibility to which we must all contribute.

## Right now

Prevention plays a leading role in eliminating or reducing the possible effects of radiological and nuclear risk, so:

- learn more about the characteristics and effects of radioactivity on health and the environment and about the measures to take in an emergency, including by consulting the section on radiological and nuclear risk on your national civil protection authority's website, where the national plan for managing radiological and nuclear emergencies can be found.

- Contact your local authorities to find out what the preventive and emergency information contains. This information is referred to in the local radiological and nuclear emergency planning for which the authorities are responsible.

## While the cloud is passing

In an emergency, follow the recommendations of the authorities (national civil protection authority, local authorities, municipalities) through official channels (websites, social media accounts, information helplines) and keep informed through national and local media.

The authorities may give instructions on the measures to follow, such as sheltering indoors, iodine prophylaxis (that is, taking potassium iodide tablets) and restrictions on eating certain foods.

## Sheltering indoors

The walls of buildings can block most of the radioactivity. As a rough guide, an instruction to shelter indoors may last 48 hours.

### If you are outdoors

- Get to an enclosed place as soon as possible.

- Make sure that those next to you are also under shelter: children, vulnerable people and your pet.

- Do not go to collect your children from school, unless the school or the authorities tell you otherwise: they are already in a safe place.

### If you are indoors

- Check whether your municipality has put in place restrictions on drinking tap water and eating the food you have at home.

- Do not eat food that has been exposed to the open air, in particular produce from vegetable plots or gardens.

- Do not leave your home or workplace.

- Close doors and windows.

- Switch off air-conditioning and systems that take in outside air.

- Close fireplaces and chimneys, if possible.

- Move to a basement or underground room, if possible.

- Use the landline or mobile phone only when strictly necessary.

- If you really have to go out, do not leave any part of your body uncovered and wear a face mask, a hat and gloves.

### When you return home or to another place

- Take off your clothes and shoes.

- Put the clothes you have taken off in a plastic bag and seal it well.

- Keep the bag out of reach of people and animals or in a separate room, to avoid radioactive contamination of your home.

- Take a shower with soap and water and wash exposed parts such as your hair and hands thoroughly.

- If you cannot take a shower, wash your eyes, ears and mouth with soap and water anyway, as soon as possible. Simply rinsing your body with water is not enough.

- Put on clean clothes.

## Health protection measures (iodine prophylaxis)

In the event of serious accidents at nearby nuclear facilities, and only for certain age groups and specific groups of the population, the public health authorities may order the taking of potassium iodide tablets (iodine prophylaxis) in order to protect the thyroid. Otherwise, taking potassium iodide may be useless and harmful.

Remember, the distribution of the tablets is activated on the basis of the criteria laid down in the national plan for managing radiological and nuclear emergencies.

- Do not buy potassium iodide tablets, whose sale is regulated, in advance or on your own initiative.

- Follow the instructions of the authorities managing the emergency through official information channels.

## Food restriction measures

The authorities may order a precautionary ban on the consumption, production and sale of local food. Follow the instructions on what you can eat and drink; in particular, the measures will concern:

- eating only packaged food, protected from radioactivity, whose food supply chain is traceable

- a ban on eating produce from local vegetable plots or fresh vegetables

- a ban on drinking milk

- restrictions, laid down in orders or notices from your municipality, on drinking the tap water you usually drink at home.

## After the cloud has passed

The food measures ordered while the cloud was passing may also remain in place afterwards, for a long period, and may change over time as the situation and the areas affected evolve.

So keep checking official channels to stay up to date on the instructions given by the authorities, including during the return to normal.
