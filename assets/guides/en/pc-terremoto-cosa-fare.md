Learning how to prevent and reduce the effects of an earthquake is a task that concerns all of us.

Share what you know with your family, at school, with friends and colleagues: spreading information about earthquake risk is a collective responsibility to which we must all contribute.

## Right now

### With the advice of an expert

Sometimes it is enough to reinforce the load-bearing walls or improve the connections between walls and floors: to make the right choice, get advice from an experienced engineer or building expert.

### On your own

- Move heavy furniture away from beds and sofas.

- Fix shelves, bookcases and other tall furniture to the walls.

- Hang pictures and mirrors with closed hooks that stop them from coming off the wall.

- Put heavy objects on the lower shelves; on the higher ones, you can secure objects with double-sided tape.

- In the kitchen, fit a catch on the doors of the cupboards that hold plates and glasses, so that they do not open during the shaking.

- Learn where the gas and water valves and the main electricity switch are, and how to turn them off.

- Prepare an emergency kit containing, for example, copies of your documents, a first aid kit, a torch, a battery-powered radio, etc.

- Identify the safe spots in your home where you can take shelter during an earthquake.

- Find out about your municipality's emergency plan.

## During the earthquake

### If you are indoors

- Stand in a doorway set in a load-bearing wall (the thickest one), next to a load-bearing wall or under a beam, or take shelter under a bed or a sturdy table.

- In the middle of the room you could be hit by falling objects, pieces of plaster, false ceilings, furniture, etc.

- Do not rush outside; wait until the shaking stops.

### If you are outdoors

- Move away from buildings, trees, street lamps and power lines: you could be hit by falling flowerpots, roof tiles and other materials.

- Watch out for the possible consequences of the earthquake: collapsed bridges, landslides, gas leaks, etc.

## After the earthquake

- Check the health of the people around you and, if necessary, give first aid.

- Before going out, turn off the gas, water and electricity and put on your shoes. On your way out, avoid the lift and be careful on the stairs, which may be damaged. Once outside, remain cautious.

- If you are in an area at risk of tsunami, move away from the beach and get to high ground.

- Use the phone as little as possible.

- Use your car as little as possible so as not to obstruct the passage of emergency vehicles.

- Go to the assembly areas set out in your municipality's emergency plan.
