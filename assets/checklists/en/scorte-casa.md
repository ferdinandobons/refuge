The European Union asks every household to be able to stay at home for at least 72 hours without shops, running water or electricity. Longer is better. Use the "Supplies" tool to calculate quantities.

## Water
- At least 3 litres per person per day for drinking, more for cooking and washing
- Sealed bottles or jerrycans, stored somewhere cool and dark
- Water purification tablets
- Bathtub and pots to fill as soon as an alert is issued

## Long-life food
- Food you can eat without cooking and with little water (tins, crackers, nuts and dried fruit)
- Pasta, rice, dried legumes
- Canned food (legumes, tuna, meat, vegetables)
- Oil, salt, sugar, honey
- UHT or powdered milk
- Baby food and pet food, if needed
- Camping stove with spare gas cartridges

## Health
- First aid kit with instructions
- Regular medicines for at least 2 weeks
- Painkillers and fever reducers
- Spare glasses and aids you use every day

## Hygiene and toilet without water
- Soap, hand sanitiser, wet wipes, toilet paper
- Toothpaste, sanitary pads, nappies
- Strong rubbish bags and a bucket with a lid
- Cat litter or sawdust to cover toilet waste

## Light, power and information
- LED torches or headlamps with spare batteries
- Candles and matches (beware of fire risk)
- Charged power banks and, if possible, a foldable solar panel
- Battery, wind-up or solar radio, with the emergency frequency written on it

## Heat and cold
- Blankets and sleeping bags
- Warm clothes: wool, thick socks, hat, gloves
- Battery fan for extreme heat

## Money and documents
- Cash in small notes and coins
- Copies of identity documents
- Important phone numbers written on paper
- Family emergency plan: contacts and meeting point

## Home
- Duct tape and plastic sheeting (to seal a room)
- Fire extinguisher
- Adjustable wrench to shut off gas and water
- Whistle to help rescuers find you
- Basic tools (hammer, screwdriver, pliers)
